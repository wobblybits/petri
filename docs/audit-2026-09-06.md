# Audit: the whole thing, at `2c1c6be`

Audited 2026-09-06 on `alife-energy`. Scope: how the frame is split across
JavaScript, wasm and the GPU; memory and allocation; dead code; bugs; and a
review of evolution, internal state and signalling for whether the design
can produce visible, open-ended change at thirty to fifty thousand bodies.

Bugs found were fixed in the same session; the fixes are listed in §3 with
what each one changes. Everything else here is a finding with a
recommendation, ranked. The earlier, narrower audit of the genome is
`signal-architecture-audit.md`; its findings are closed and this does not
repeat them.

Two memory notes from earlier sessions are load-bearing and were confirmed:
the shipping path is `stepAsync`, not `step`, and only it can use the GPU;
and the soup at the shipped `declutter`/`flockAlign` barely breeds.

---

## 1. Summary

**The frame at 10,000 bodies is 78.6 ms on the GPU path**, measured in
Chrome after the fixes below, with the ledger in §11. Three passes carry
half of it: the constraint solve (15.5), the latch pass (10.6) and the force
block pack (8.8). None of them is arithmetic-bound; all three are JS walking
objects. The 50k target needs the solve to stay in wasm (its cap is 32,768
bodies) and the two JS passes to stop being per-body object work. §8 has the
plan.

**Nothing has been shown to evolve.** The ledger and the census agree on why:
at the shipped settings a 10k soup makes about 400 commutes in its first
second and then the meeting rate collapses; `bornMean` after 45 s is 0.36.
Selection acts through reproduction, and reproduction is gated by a physical
meeting the shoaling and spacing forces prevent. §7 argues that the single
largest lever for visible evolution is not in the genome at all but in where
new bodies come from, and proposes the change.

**Fourteen bugs were fixed**, five of them in the GPU path that ships. The
worst: a preset reload on a GPU machine paid the old pond's harvest and
genome results to the new pond's bodies, and left the old pond's scent in the
device buffers. The second worst: killing a body scanned every wire in the
pond, twice per rewrite and once per death, which at 70k wires is most of a
frame.

**Dead code removed:** the threaded confine worker pool, 400 lines and a
second wasm blob, referenced by nothing.

## 2. How the frame is split

```
                JS (sim.ts)                wasm (solver.c)            GPU (wgsl)
beginFrame  ┌ components, home, pin
            │ openForceBlock ────pack──▶ bodies[12n] invI kind scale mass
            │ refreshForceScratch ─────▶ flags pwire comp sat portFree   (cached on topology)
            │ refreshBound
            │ steer ────────────────────▶ solver_steer (samples in from store)
            │ portTorques ──────────────▶ solver_port_torques
            │ declutter ────────────────▶ solver_declutter
            │ flock ────────────────────▶ solver_flock (pair cache on topology)
            │ LOD, rope paths, rope shape
solve       │ FAR: ─────────────────────▶ solver_step_far  (≤32,768 bodies)
            │      or ───────────────────────────────────────────────▶ far.wgsl
            │ NEAR: ────────────────────▶ solver_step_near (XPBD ropes, SAT)
            │      or JS twin
endFrame    │ syncForces, damp, lengths, snap-taut
            │ harvest (credit) ◀───────────────────────────────────── harvest (last frame)
            │ graph.snap (latch)
            │ collectReadyRedexes, pulseRequests (need field, flow, state), escrow, rewrites
            │ swimCost, farming, contact damage, upkeep → kill
            │ tuneChannels
            │ CPU field: deposit ───────▶ solver_deposit (box copy in/out)
            │            diffuse ×2, react, decay, grow  (JS, or wasm ≤65k cells)
            │ autoSpawn
gpuFieldStep│ plan harvest, pack deposits/probes/blocks ──────────────▶ field.wgsl:
            │                                                            fill, scatter, applyAcc,
            │                                                            diffuse, diffuse2, react,
            │                                                            decay, grow, harvest, gather
            │ pack h, inputs, CSR, chem(dirty) ──────────────────────▶ genome.wgsl: state
            │ ◀── samples[8n], harvest got, genome out[18n] ───────────
            └ unpack into store by slot
```

What crosses per frame, and what is duplicated:

- **JS → wasm, every frame:** the pose pack (12 f32 a body) once, shared by
  every force pass through the force block; per-pass metadata that is
  cached on topology (steer flags, principal wire, component, saturation,
  free-port mask, flock CSR and pair list). The taste vector (4 f32) and
  cruise/turn cross every frame because the genome recomputes them.
- **JS → GPU, every frame:** deposits (8 f32 per port, three ports a body),
  probes (12 f32 a body), harvest blocks and rooms, the genome's `h` (4), its
  inputs (8), and the CSR (`n+1 + 2W` u32). The genome table itself now
  crosses only for the slots born since the last frame.
- **GPU → JS, every frame:** 8 f32 a body of samples, one f32 per hungry
  body of harvest, 18 f32 a body of genome outputs. One round trip: the
  genome dispatch is queued behind the field's and both readbacks are
  awaited together (this session; it was two).
- **Duplicated representations of the wire graph:** `flockAdj` (array of
  arrays, JS), the wake-graph CSR (JS), the wasm `adj_off/adj_nei`, and
  `WireAdjacency` — four CSRs of the same edges, each rebuilt on its own
  schedule. §8.3.
- **Duplicated id→index maps:** `packIndex`, `farIndex`, `scratchIndex`,
  `flockIndex`, `wakeIndex`, `adjIndex` — six maps from agent id to list
  position, all describing the same list. §8.3.
- **Duplicated field storage:** on the GPU path the CPU `Fields` (16 MB ×2)
  and the wasm scent mirror (16 MB ×2, static) are both dead weight except
  for the debug overlay. §5.

The split itself is sound. Physics and per-body arithmetic are in wasm;
stencil work and the embarrassingly parallel genome are on the GPU; topology,
the economy and the rewrite calculus are in JS. What is wrong is not where
things run but how much of the JS layer is per-body object work — `Map`
lookups by id, `Agent` accessor calls, arrays of records rebuilt per frame —
around passes that take a millisecond.

## 3. Bugs fixed this session

Each is verified by reading, by the suite (759 → 759 passing, the same three
pre-existing failures), and where it touches the GPU path, in Chrome.

| # | Where | What was wrong | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `graph.ts` `detachAgent` | Copied the entire wire map to an array and scanned it, per call | O(W) per death and 2×O(W) per rewrite. At 70k wires and tens of deaths a frame this is most of a frame | Three port lookups |
| 2 | `sim.ts` `clear()` | `harvestPending`, `genomePending`, the genome slot buffer and the device-side field survived a preset reload | On a GPU machine the old pond's harvest and genome results were paid to the new pond's bodies (ids restart at 1, slots at 0, so the id guard matched), and the old scent stayed in the buffers | Reset all of it; `FieldGpu.clear()` zeroes the three device buffers with `clearBuffer` |
| 3 | `energy.ts` `EnergyGrid.clear()` | The deferred add queue and pending seed survived | Deaths in the last frame of an old pond landed on the new pond's ground | Reset both |
| 4 | `sim.ts` `gpuFieldStep`/`steerNative` | Steering samples were written into the solver's array in *list* order at the end of one frame and read at the top of the next | A spawn or erase between the two shifted every later body onto its neighbour's sensor readings; a body born past the old end steered on stale buffer contents | `AgentStore.steerAll` by slot; packed to list order at steer time |
| 5 | `sim.ts` `steerNative` | `loadScent` ran unconditionally, copying the field's live box into wasm | On the GPU path the box is the whole disk: a ~12 MB memcpy a frame that the solver then never read, since `use_samples` was on | Skipped when steering from samples |
| 6 | `sim.ts` `gpuGenomeStep` | Uploaded the whole genome table every frame | 2.7 MB/frame at 5k, 27 MB at 50k | `AgentStore.chemVersion` + dirty slot range, stamped in `refreshReadsField` (the existing chem-write choke point) and `clearSlot`; a frame with one birth uploads one genome |
| 7 | `sim.ts` | Genome dispatch waited for the field readback before being submitted | Two GPU round trips a frame | `FieldGpu`/`GenomeGpu` split into `submit`/`collect`; both readbacks awaited together |
| 8 | `agents.ts` `momentOfInertia` | Built the three triangle vertices and summed them | `packPose` asks for it once a body a frame: four objects a body, ~200k a frame at 50k | Closed form, `TRI_VERTEX_R2` |
| 9 | `agents.ts` `createAgent` | `cruise`/`turn` were not seeded (flockAlign/flockSep were) | A fresh soup stood still for one frame and a newborn could not steer until the state pass ran | Seeded from the sliders |
| 10 | `graph.ts` `snap` | Built an object with a fresh `PortRef` per free port and a record per candidate, every frame | ~100k short-lived objects a frame at 50k bodies | Parallel typed arrays reused across frames; `PortRef`s only for the latches actually made; `inSnapArcAt` without the three allocating helpers |
| 11 | `energy.ts` `HarvestPlan.build`, `take` | `index()` record per body, `Map` of arrays per occupied cell, `decodeKey`/`blockRect` records per block | ~100k allocations a frame at 50k | Intrusive lists over the field's own dense cell table; `blockRectAt` into scratch |
| 12 | `sim.ts` rewrite phase | `pulseRequests`, `accrueRedexes`, `startRewrites` each walked every wire, resolved both ends and measured the rope to find the same ready set; `rewriteBusy()` built a `Set` per pass | Three full wire walks a frame | `collectReadyRedexes` once; `rewriteFrozen` is the busy set |
| 13 | `sim.ts` | `WireAdjacency` built twice a frame on the GPU path with two id maps | Two CSR builds and ~300k `Map.get` a frame at 50k | `wireAdjacency()` keyed on graph and roster versions, shared by the need field and the genome |
| 14 | `sim.ts`, `graph.ts` | `slotsFor()` allocates; called per body in `refreshBound`, `scentWriteNative`, `deposit`, `isWired`, `portsFilled`, `reelRewriteLeftovers` | 50k array allocations a frame in each | The frozen `ERA_SLOTS`/`NODE_SLOTS`; `freePortMask` helper |

Also: `Fields.spans()` built a template-string cache stamp five times a
frame (six numeric compares now); `PairGrid.build` accepts typed arrays.

### Verified in Chrome, 10k-body soup, GPU field and genome on

After 40 hand-driven frames: every body has a normalised emit vector (mean
sum 1.000), 9,984 of 9,985 bodies have non-zero steering samples, `h[0]`
saturates at exactly `phi(1) = 0.5` for hungry bodies, the genome table
version matches the store's with an empty dirty range, 434 rewrites in
flight, no console errors. After clicking Reset: the device field reads zero
cells non-zero, every pending flag is down, the seed is queued, one frame
lays 867,724 ground cells, and thirty frames later the pond is breeding
again with the genome synced.

## 4. Bugs and hazards found, not fixed

These need either a decision or work larger than a session, and are ranked
by consequence.

**4.1 `peakSpeed` in the thousands during the first two seconds of a large
soup.** A 10k soup reports a fastest body between 1,700 and 3,800 px/s over
its first hundred frames, on `2c1c6be` and on this build alike (measured
back to back, §11). Bodies spawn overlapping in a disk and the disc contact
resolves the pile violently; this is the "flung apart" of the memory note,
and it is spawn overlap, not the solver. `loadPreset` should place bodies on
a jittered lattice or reject positions closer than a body diameter, which is
cheap and removes the transient entirely. Worth doing before any experiment
that reads the first thirty seconds.

**4.2 Three pre-existing test failures are not flakes.** They fail alone,
every run, and two of them assert the same thing: `determinism > gives the
same state twice` fails because a fully-FAR scene and a mixed-LOD scene hash
*identically*, which the test correctly reads as "a pass is not running";
`oscillator-zoom > settles the same net to the same shape` fails because the
FAR and close-up runs disagree by 18 px on an aux gap. Both point at the
NEAR/FAR tier boundary — either the detailed tier is not engaging in the
mixed scene, or it is and does not match. `connected > oscillator net`
reports a peak speed of 30.5 against a bound of 24 on a settled net. The
memory note says not to chase these; they are listed here because a
correctness audit should say what they are: **the physics LOD line is not
producing the same net on both sides, and the suite has been saying so.**

**4.3 The wasm body cap is 32,768 and the target is 50,000.** Above the cap
the FAR solve goes to the GPU kernel, which the memory note measured at
about twice wasm's time at 6k bodies. `MAX_BODIES` is a `#define` and the
derived statics (`native/caps.test.ts`) are ~60 MB; doubling it needs ~30 MB
more and `INITIAL_MEMORY` raised. The scent mirror in wasm (32 MB) is dead on
the GPU path and could be dropped from a GPU-path build to pay for it. §8.1.

**4.4 `agentList` is rebuilt by six different methods** (`forceList`,
`solve`, `packFar`, `solveNearNative`, `rebuildBodyGrid`, `assignPhysicsLod`)
from the same `Map` in the same order, and only `forceList` stamps
`listRoster`. They agree today because `Map` iteration order is insertion
order and ids are monotonic. Nothing enforces it. `wireAdjacency()` and the
GPU unpack both assume `forceList()` order; a future pass that sorts or
filters the shared array corrupts both silently.

**4.5 `Agent.sense` is scaled in place** by `updateState` after the GPU
probe writes it raw. The accessor's doc says raw. Any second reader gets the
scaled values on the GPU path and raw on the CPU path (where `sampleAll`
rewrites them each frame). Give the scaling its own array or scale at the
read.

**4.6 `Sim.contacts` and `contactAudioNow` are keyed on template strings**
(`"lo:hi"`), one allocation per SAT contact per substep. Only the NEAR tier
pays it, so at 50k zoomed out it is nothing, and zoomed in on a few hundred
bodies it is fine. Numeric keys (`lo * 2^21 + hi`) if it ever shows in the
ledger.

**4.7 `componentIds` and `hopDistances` cache on `agents.size`** as well as
the graph version. A kill plus a spawn of two loners keeps the size and the
version, so the cached map holds a dead id and lacks a live one. Harmless for
components (a missing loner reads as its own component), wrong for
`hopDistances` (test-only). Key on `rosterVersion`.

**4.8 The tally of rewrites by rule, births and deaths did not exist.** The
census can say how deep the population is, not how fast it turns over. Added
this session as `Sim.tally` for the experiment harness (§9).

## 5. Memory

Per body, the store holds 47 fields: 41 f64 (328 B), `chem` 134 f32
(536 B), `h` 4 f64, `sense` 4 f64, `steer` 3 f64, `emit`/`taste` 8 f64
(152 B), plus the `Agent` flyweight and up to three cached `subarray` views
(~400 B of JS objects). About **1.4 KB a body**; 70 MB at 50k. The
`Map<number, Agent>` roster is another ~2 MB. Fine.

What is not fine is the fixed cost:

| | size | live on the GPU path? |
|---|---|---|
| `Fields.data` + `tmp` | 32 MB | only for the debug overlay readback |
| `Fields.scroll` (lazy) | 16 MB | never (the world no longer scrolls) |
| wasm `scent` + `scent_tmp` | 32 MB | never |
| wasm everything else | ~33 MB | yes |
| GPU field ×3 | 48 MB | yes |
| `nativeSolver` wasm heap | 80 MB total | |

**Every `Sim` allocates 32 MB of field it may never touch**, and the test
suite constructs hundreds of them. `Sim` should take a field size
(`new Sim(w, h, { fieldCells })`) with the default 1024 and tests at 128;
that alone is 16× less allocation per test and is the cheapest thing in this
document to do for suite speed.

`Fields.scroll` is dead: `cover` is called once from `pinWorld` and the
world is pinned for life. Delete `shiftCells` and the lazy buffer.

The wasm scent mirror is only used when the field is on the CPU. Two builds
(with and without `MAX_SCENT`) or a runtime-sized allocation would free 32 MB
on the shipping path, which is exactly what raising `MAX_BODIES` needs.

## 6. Dead code

Removed this session:

- `src/native/confine-pool.ts`, `confine-worker.ts`, `confine-threaded.wasm`,
  `confine-threaded.b64.ts`, `native/build-confine-threaded.sh`, and
  `NativeSolver.confine`. A worker pool for `solver_confine_range`,
  referencing a `Sim.runConfineLoop` that does not exist. Nothing imported
  it. `solver_confine` and `solver_confine_range` are still in `solver.c`
  and the export list, unreferenced; dropping them wants a wasm rebuild and
  a parity run, so they are left for the next rebuild.
- `decodeKey`, `rewriteBusy`, the string span stamp.

Still present, of no use:

- `Fields.cover`/`shiftCells`/`scroll` — the world is pinned once (§5).
- `NativeSolver.scentDiffuse`/`scentDecay` decline any grid over 65k cells,
  and the grid is a million. They run for nothing but the field-kernel tests.
- `Sim.step`, the synchronous twin. Forty test files call it, so it stays,
  but it is a second copy of the frame with its own marker set and it is the
  one nobody ships. A `stepSync` that shares `frameBody(kind)` with
  `stepAsync` would end the duplication; the memory note about missing phase
  markers is what that duplication already cost once.
- `harvestSlots`, `tickUpkeep`, `resetRequests`, `spreadRequests`,
  `flowCharges` — the object-shaped twins of the `Fast` store versions,
  kept for `energy.test.ts`. Five algorithms written twice. The tests could
  build a store (`createAgent` with no store argument makes one) and the
  twins go.
- `Graph.hopDistances` — "kept for tests and debug".
- `Agent` accessors for `integVx/integVy/integOmega` — stored, never read
  by anything but `cloneAgent`.

## 7. Evolution, internal state, signalling

### 7.1 What is there

A body's genome is 134 floats: two 4-vectors of bases (`e0`, `t0`) that say
what it emits and what it seeks; a 4-dimensional recurrent state `h` updated
once a frame from seven inputs (four channel readings, own fullness, fraction
of ports attached, the net's demand), its own last state, and the mean of its
wired neighbours' last state; and five linear read-out heads on `h` for emit,
taste, flocking, transport and locomotion. Every weight is bounded; `phi`
keeps `h` in (−1, 1); the emit vector is normalised to a unit budget.

This is a **one-layer recurrent graph neural network with mean aggregation,
run at 60 Hz over a graph whose edges are the interaction net's wires**, with
inheritance at commutes (blend or assort per gene, then mutate) and at
erasures (clone and mutate). It is a coherent thing. The design record in
`chem-layout.ts` is accurate.

### 7.2 Where it is incoherent

**(a) Reproduction is decoupled from every heritable trait.** A commute
happens when a Con and a Dup meet principal to principal and can pay. Meeting
is physics; paying is being fed. Neither depends on the genome except
through chemotaxis toward the right channel. The census says `bornMean`
under 0.4 at a minute; the shipped `declutter` and `flockAlign` cost
eighteen times the generation depth (memory note, confirmed by the params
comment). **The pond has mutation, inheritance and death, but almost no
reproduction, so it has almost no selection.** No change to the genome
matters until this does.

**(b) Most of the genome is neutral most of the time.** Taste and the
locomotion head act only on a free principal; a wired body does not steer.
Flocking acts on everyone; transport on wired bodies. So a body in a mature
net has 56 of its 134 genes inert, and a loner has the other 24 inert. With
every gene mutated every birth at `CHEM_MUTATE ≈ 0.021`, the inert ones walk.
`matrixDrift` in the harness will rise whether or not anything is selected.

**(c) The three signal channels are physically identical.** Same diffuse,
same decay. A lineage can choose *which* channel to speak on but the
channels afford no different meanings — there is no "fast and local" versus
"slow and far". `diffuseRate`/`decayRate` per channel already exist and are
all ones for the signals.

**(d) Survival cost is flat.** `upkeep` is the same for every body; `swimCost`
and `contactCost` ship at zero. A body that swims fast at random pays what a
body that sits still pays. Income is where a body stands; there is no
behaviour that costs anything. The locomotion head is therefore unselectable
in the only way that matters (speed against fuel).

**(e) Eras mint.** `ERA_UPKEEP_RATIO = −0.2` makes energy from a kind rule,
which the project's own stated principle ("nothing about the three kinds
should be true by fiat except what the calculus requires") argues against,
now that logistic ground regrowth is the world's producer.

**(f) Immigration resets the gene pool.** `autoSpawn` places fresh seed
genomes at a third each kind. Under near-zero reproduction, the population's
genome is dominated by immigrants and the census `lines` count stays near the
number that arrived.

**(g) Nothing is visible.** Evolution could be happening and nobody would
see it: bodies are coloured by kind, and the only readout is `census()` from
a console.

### 7.3 Proposals, in order of leverage

**P1. Spawn from the living population, not the seed.** `params.spawnFrom:
'seed' | 'population'`. In population mode `autoSpawn` picks a parent
weighted by `extra` (or uniformly among bodies above break-even), clones its
genome and traits with the erase-mutation, and places the child near the
parent. This is outside the calculus — spawning already is — so it costs
nothing in purity, and it turns immigration from dilution into the
reproduction the pond otherwise lacks. It is also the standard move in
artificial-life systems that have shown sustained adaptation (Tierra's and
Avida's reproduction is an explicit copy step; Flow-Lenia's is mass
splitting). One afternoon; the experiment in §9 measures it.

**P2. Turn `swimCost` on** at the value the params comment computes
(0.0004 roughly doubles a mover's bill). Then cruise is a real trade and the
`L` head is under selection. Sweep it with the harness first (§9).

**P3. Make the channels different substances.** Seed `diffuseRate` and
`decayRate` so `CH.conP` is fast and short (a presence), `CH.dupP` slow and
long (a trail), `CH.aux` as is. No genome change; the vocabulary doubles.

**P4. Mutate sparsely.** Replace "every gene ± `CHEM_MUTATE`" with "each
gene mutates with probability `k / CHEM_LEN`, `k ≈ 3`, by a larger step".
Same expected displacement, far less drift in the neutral genes, and a
lineage can hold a working configuration. This is the NEAT/ES default and
the drift-barrier argument applies directly at these population sizes.

**P5. Colour bodies by what they say.** The emit vector is a unit-sum
4-vector; map `conP, dupP, aux` to RGB. Evolution of signalling becomes
something you watch. Add `colorBy: 'kind' | 'emit' | 'lineage'` to the
render options. Half a day, and it is the thing that makes the project's
goal observable.

**P6. Retire the Era mint** once P1 and P2 are in (`ERA_UPKEEP_RATIO = 0`).
The ground is the producer; an Era's job is to be somewhere useful, which
farming and fertilising already reward.

**P7. Fold the five scalar traits into the genome** as bounded genes with
their own range map. One inheritance path instead of two (`inheritTraits`
and `inheritChem`), one mutation rule, one thing the harness measures.
Low priority; it is tidiness, not leverage.

**P8. Record lineage properly.** `born`/`lineage` exist. Add a
`Chronicle`: a ring buffer of `census()` plus `tally` every N seconds, and a
per-lineage genome centroid. That is the data for a phylogeny and the input
to any evolutionary-activity statistic (§10).

What to leave alone: `DEMAND` fixed-function (the hoarding argument in the
memory note is right); the emit budget; bounded weights; mean aggregation;
`phi`. The recurrent-GNN shape is the right one.

## 8. Getting to 30–50k bodies

At 10k the frame is 79 ms. Fifty thousand at 30 fps needs the whole frame
under 33 ms, so a factor of twelve at five times the bodies: everything
per-body has to be near-free in JS or not in JS.

### 8.1 Keep the solve in wasm

`solve` is 15.5 ms at 10k and scales linearly. Raise `MAX_BODIES` to 65,536
(and `MAX_WIRES`, `MAX_NODES`, `MAX_PAIRS`, `MAX_FLOCK_PAIRS` with it),
`INITIAL_MEMORY` to ~128 MB, and drop the scent mirror from the GPU-path
build. Re-run `native/caps.test.ts`. Confirm with `frame-profile` that the
FAR solve at 50k is ~40 ms and then attack it: the NEAR/FAR split already
exists, and at 50k zoomed out everything is FAR, so the solve is
`integrate + disc + span + finalize + wall` — `disc` is the spatial hash,
which is where the time goes. Worker threads (the `SharedArrayBuffer` build
that the deleted pool proved out) would split `disc` and `span` across four
cores; that is the one place threading pays here.

### 8.2 Move the latch pass off objects

`snap` is 10.6 ms at 10k and was the second phase. This session removed its
allocations; what remains is `forEachPair` over ~30k free ports with a
closure per pair, `latchCrosses` per candidate, and `buildLatchIndex` over
every wire every frame. Two moves: port the pair loop to wasm (it is the same
spatial hash `solver_steer` already runs, over the same bodies, so the
solver could emit candidate pairs into a buffer), and rebuild the latch
index only when a wire is born or dies (its boxes move every frame, but a
box grown by the wire's rest length is valid until the topology changes).

### 8.3 One roster, one adjacency, slot-keyed everything

The JS layer keys on agent id and resolves through `Map`s: six id→index
maps, four CSRs, and every wire endpoint is `{id, slot}` resolved with
`agents.get`. Replace with: a dense `liveSlots: Int32Array` in `AgentStore`
kept in `Map` order (bumped with `rosterVersion`); `Wire` gains cached store
slots for its two ends, refreshed on roster change; one CSR over slots in the
store, rebuilt on `(graph.version, rosterVersion)`, consumed by flocking, the
wake graph, the need field, the genome and the wasm pack. Every
`this.agents.get(w.a.id)` in a hot loop becomes an array read. This is the
refactor with the widest payoff and the least risk, because every consumer
already goes through one of the caches it replaces.

### 8.4 Stop packing what does not change

`packPose` writes mass, inertia, kind and scale every frame. Only pose
changes. Split the pack into a topology/roster part (done on version bump)
and a pose part. Same for `packNearMeta`.

### 8.5 Field: the cost is fixed and already paid

The field is 3 ms of GPU time at any body count; deposits and probes are
linear in bodies and now allocation-free. Nothing to do.

### 8.6 What to measure

`src/native/frame-profile.perf.test.ts` drives `step`; the shipping path is
`stepAsync` in a browser. Add a browser bench page (`bench.html`) that loads
a soup of N, drives `stepAsync` by hand for 200 frames with `Sim.profile`
on, and prints the ledger — the loop this audit used from the console. The
Browser pane does not composite, so `requestAnimationFrame` never fires;
drive by hand.

## 9. Testing

### 9.1 What is there

762 tests in 60 files, 261 s wall on this machine, three of which fail every
run (§4.2) and two of which are order-dependent. Every `Sim` allocates 32 MB
of field. `net-health.test.ts` and `determinism.test.ts` each run minutes of
simulated pond; `determinism` alone is 140 s. The audio suite is 2,900 lines
and unrelated to the pond.

### 9.2 Reorganise by what a test costs

Four vitest projects, by cost, not by module:

| project | include | runs |
|---|---|---|
| `unit` | pure functions: layout, energy arithmetic, geometry, grid, rewrite rules, kernel mirrors, audio | every save, seconds |
| `sim` | integration under a second of simulated time each | every commit, a minute |
| `soak` | `net-health`, `determinism`, `connected`, `oscillator-zoom`, minute-long runs | on demand and CI, ten minutes |
| `bench` | `*.perf.test.ts` | on demand |
| `experiments` | `*.exp.ts`, the harness below | by hand |

`npm test` runs `unit` and `sim`. Selection by file list rather than
renaming: the long files are named in `vite.config.ts`.

Three things make the suite less flaky at no cost: reset `nativeSolver`'s
caches in `test-setup` (`fkSim`, `skSim`, `scentOwner`, `use_samples`) the
way `Sim` statics are already reset, since the wasm module is a singleton
and the order dependence lives there; give `Sim` a `fieldCells` option and
build test ponds at 128; and mark the three §4.2 failures `it.fails` with
the reason in the name so a green run means something again.

### 9.3 The experiment harness (added)

`src/experiments/harness.ts` runs a seeded pond for N simulated seconds and
samples it: population, lines, generation depth, the tally of commutes,
erases, annihilations, births, deaths, latches; energy in bodies, ground and
escrow; how many bodies can pay; principal-to-principal and Con–Dup wire
counts; emit and taste base means and spreads; matrix drift; the heritable
scalars. `runSweep` crosses a parameter grid with seeds and writes JSON to
`experiments/out/`.

Two experiments ship with it: `breeding.exp.ts` (declutter × flockAlign ×
spawnInterval) and `economy.exp.ts` (each zero-shipped dial alone). Run with
`npm run experiment -- breeding`. The regime for tuning is:

1. **Meeting.** Sweep the spacing forces until `commutes/second` per body is
   in a range where `bornMean` climbs past 2 within three minutes.
2. **Economy.** With meeting fixed, sweep `swimCost`, then `farmRate`,
   `forageAsk`, `fertilise`, one at a time, reading `died`, `canPay` and
   `bornMean` together. A dial that raises deaths without raising depth is
   a tax, not a pressure.
3. **Heritability.** With both fixed, run three seeds for ten minutes and
   read `lines` against `bornMean`: lines falling while depth climbs is
   selection. Then `emitSd`/`tasteSd` by channel says whether the population
   is diverging in what it says.
4. **Regression.** Freeze the winning point as a `sim`-project test that
   asserts `bornMean > 1` at two minutes, seeded. That is the first test in
   the repo that would fail if evolution stopped.

## 10. What the literature says, and what applies

- **Flow-Lenia** (Plantec et al., *Artificial Life* 2025) got sustained
  evolutionary dynamics out of a continuous CA by adding two things Lenia
  lacked: mass conservation, and parameters embedded in the substrate so
  they are carried and mixed by the dynamics. Petri already has both — the
  energy economy is conservative by design, and the genome rides on bodies.
  What Flow-Lenia measures its success with is the **evolutionary activity
  statistics** (Bedau et al.): track each genotype's persistence weighted by
  its population, and separate "new adaptations keep appearing" from
  "diversity is bounded". §7.3 P8 is the data for that.
- **Minimal criterion coevolution** (Brant & Stanley, GECCO 2017, and the
  2019 benchmark): open-ended search needs no fitness function, only a
  minimal criterion — survive long enough to reproduce — and diverges on its
  own once the criterion is satisfiable by many routes. Petri's criterion is
  exactly that, and P1 is what makes it satisfiable at all.
- **Honest signalling.** The 2022 BMC Biology result (Számadó et al.) is
  that honesty is maintained by trade-offs rather than costs, which is the
  argument the emit budget already makes and `emitCost`'s deletion relied on.
  The artificial-world study of inter-agent signalling (Werner & Dyer's line,
  *Artificial Life* 7(1)) found signalling evolves when a signal is both
  useful to kin and costly to the signaller through the attention it draws;
  P3 (channels with different reach) is what gives a signal a range to be
  costly over.
- **Neural cellular automata and graph NCA** (Grattarola et al. NeurIPS
  2021; the 2025 self-organising GNCA work with adaptive edges): Petri's
  state update is a graph NCA whose edges are rewritten by the calculus. The
  relevant lesson from that literature is about *training*: they learn the
  update rule with gradients against a target. Petri evolves it instead,
  which is fine, but it means the update needs many generations, which
  returns to P1.
- **Mutation rates.** NEAT-style systems mutate a small fraction of weights
  per offspring with per-weight probability; evolution-strategy work on
  recurrent controllers used σ ≈ 0.06 with decay on populations of fifty.
  The drift-barrier argument says the effective rate a population can
  tolerate falls with population size and with the number of neutral loci.
  Petri mutates 134 loci every birth; P4.
- **Interaction combinators as artificial chemistry** (Buliga, 2020):
  directed interaction combinators and chemlambda were compared for alife
  properties and the finding was that graph rewrite systems that *allow
  conflicting rewrites* do better at replication and metabolism than
  deterministic ones. Petri's rewrites are deterministic once a redex is
  ready; the escrow and the physical meeting are where the non-determinism
  lives, which is a reasonable place for it.

Sources: Flow-Lenia [MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/artl/article/31/2/228/130572/Flow-Lenia-Emergent-Evolutionary-Dynamics-in-Mass),
[arXiv 2506.08569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08569);
MCC [GECCO 2017](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3071178.3071186),
